First things first: what is radiation?

We keep radiation under control, too.

Radio waves, heat, light: it's all radiation. At the far end of the spectrum, and from radioactive atoms, it becomes ionising: energetic enough to knock electrons out of atoms. Sounds scary, but in the hospital we use radiation deliberately: to look inside your body and to destroy tumours. Always following the ALARA principle: as low as reasonably achievable.

  • αAlpha helium nucleus, stopped by paper
  • βBeta electron, a few mm of aluminium
  • γGamma & X-rays photon, lead or concrete

The idea

What actually happens behind the doors of a hospital when someone is diagnosed with cancer?

In Mission: tumour under control you follow the patient journey of a cancer patient: from the first scan to the last radiotherapy session. You visit three stations, each showing one step of the diagnostic and therapeutic pathway.

Along the way you meet the people you hardly ever see: the medical physicists. They are the scientists behind MRI, CT, PET-CT and radiotherapy machines, and they make sure every patient gets the right dose, in the right place, to the millimetre.

01

Station 1 · Diagnosis

Seeing without cutting

Diagnostic and functional imaging: on a large screen and with the VERTUAL system we show how MRI, CT and PET-CT work. Why does a CT show bones so sharply, while an MRI shows the soft tissues? And how does a PET-CT reveal where a tumour is active?

Here you meet two kinds of medical physicists: medical physicists in diagnostic imaging, who ensure sharp images with as little dose as possible in X-ray (RX) and CT, and safe and reliable MRI scans; and medical physicists in nuclear medicine, who look after the radioactive tracers and the PET and gamma cameras.

  • Walk around a patient in 3D and look right through the body
  • Discover what each scanner ‘sees’ and why we combine them
  • See how doctors and physicists outline the tumour before treatment

Station 2 · Treatment

You're at the controls

Radiotherapy in practice: an interactive demonstration with the VERTUAL system, a life-size virtual radiotherapy bunker. Visitors experience first-hand how a patient is positioned and how a treatment is delivered. We are already in contact with VERTUAL's founder and CSO.

Here you meet the medical physicists in radiotherapy: they make sure the treatment plan is right, check the machines and see to it that the dose lands precisely in the tumour, while sparing the healthy tissue as much as possible.

  • Position the patient to the millimetre with lasers and a robotic couch
  • Rotate the machine around the patient
  • See how the beam hits the tumour and spares healthy tissue

↳ Part of the treatment

Make your own mask, with Belgian company ORFIT. During radiotherapy the patient's head stays perfectly still thanks to a custom-made thermoplastic mask. At the festival, children make such a mask themselves: they shape a small piece of ORFIT material over a doll's head, just as it is done for a real patient in the hospital.

Who are we?

BHPA is the Belgian Hospital Physicists Association (BVZF · SBPH).

We are scientists who ensure the correct and safe use of technology and ionising radiation in medicine: in radiology, MRI, nuclear medicine and radiotherapy, and through radiation protection. You may know us as medical physicists.

We are the bridge between doctor, patient and technology, in diagnosis as well as in treatment. Our most important task: guaranteeing that medical imaging and radiation treatments are accurate and safe, for the patient and for everyone working in the hospital. That way, every patient gets the best possible chance of recovery.
